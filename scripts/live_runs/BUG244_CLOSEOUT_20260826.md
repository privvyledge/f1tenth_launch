## 2 · bug-248 — root cause found. It is a *timestamp* defect, not an attitude defect

Three claims in the previous write-up are wrong and are corrected here.

**(a) It is not "~10 s into a cold launch". It is at IMU stream start.**
Anchored to the first `/camera/imu/filtered` sample instead of the bag start, the jump lands at
**+0.02 to +0.10 s** in all four launches examined. The "~10 s" was the camera's own startup
delay (`camera_launch_delay`, 6 s, plus driver enumeration). The standing speculation that the
timing "sits near localization's `TimerAction(period=10s)`" is therefore **refuted** — the two
clocks only looked related.

**(b) It is not intermittent.** It is present in **4 of 4** launches examined, including the
three that did not diverge. What was ~3-in-8 was whether the jump landed while `ekf_odom` was
fusing, not whether it occurred.

| run | jump | at | `hdr_dt` on that step | next largest step in the run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| run12 | 82.56 deg | +0.05 s | +27.21 s | 0.91 deg (91x) |
| run14 | 63.18 then 80.63 deg | +0.07, +0.10 s | +1252.60 s, then -1153.10 s | 1.22 deg |
| run15 | 123.37 deg | +0.09 s | +33.27 s | 0.51 deg (240x) |
| run16 | 166.63 deg | +0.02 s | +27.39 s | 0.63 deg (263x) |

**(c) The sensor stream is clean; the discontinuity is manufactured downstream.**
Across the very interval in which the filtered attitude moved:

| run | filtered attitude moved | rotation the RAW gyro reports over the same interval | raw accel direction change |
|---|---|---|---|
| run14 | 80.63 deg | **0.000 deg** | 0.000 deg (abs a 9.827 -> 9.827 m/s^2) |
| run16 | 166.63 deg | **0.003 deg** | 2.535 deg (abs a 9.838 -> 9.712 m/s^2) |

The car is parked on a desk. Raw rates are 0.005-0.03 rad/s and the accel vector holds gravity
steady on -y throughout. Nothing in the measured data rotates.

**The mechanism.** The RealSense driver stamps its first 2-3 IMU samples with header times that
are seconds away from the rest — the `hdr_dt` column above, up to +1252.6 s followed by
-1153.1 s. `imu_filter_madgwick` passes those stamps straight through (raw and filtered carry
byte-identical `hdr_dt` values at those samples) and, with **`constant_dt: 0.0`**, derives its
integration timestep from them. A multi-second — or negative — dt makes one filter update
arbitrary, and the attitude steps by 63-167 deg in a single sample. It then re-converges over
a few seconds as the accel correction pulls it back to gravity. That re-convergence window is
where bug-244's ~1 g gravity-removal residual came from.

Verified live on the node: `/realsense_imu_filter` has `constant_dt = 0.0`, `gain = 0.3`.

**Fix applied and verified: `constant_dt` 0.0 -> 0.005 s on the RealSense madgwick node.**

It is passed as a new launch argument, `imu_filter_constant_dt`, rather than hardcoded, because
`launch/filters/imu_filter.launch.py` is shared with the VESC IMU chain, which runs at ~100 Hz —
a hardcoded 0.005 would silently mis-time that filter. The argument **defaults to `0.0`** (current
behaviour, header-derived dt) and only `launch/sensors/realsense_d435i.launch.py` sets `0.005`,
matching `gyro_fps: 200` and the measured 198.9-199.9 Hz. It is applied to both madgwick blocks in
the file, so the with-bias-correction path stays in sync for when `ros-humble-imu-pipeline` lands.

`config/filters/imu_filter.yaml` already read `constant_dt: 0.0` and **was not the place to change
it** — the launch file passes explicit parameter dicts and its `parameters=[imu_filter_param_file]`
line is commented out, so that YAML is not loaded at all. A comment now says so in the file, since
this is the same trap `NEXT_CHAT_IMU_BIAS.md` flags for `do_bias_estimation`/`gain_acc`/`gain_mag`.

**Verification — `run17_constdt`, same 240 s cold-launch procedure:**

| | before (run14/15/16) | after (run17) |
|---|---|---|
| `constant_dt` on `/realsense_imu_filter` | 0.0 | **0.005** (read back live as a Double) |
| largest attitude step in the run | 80.63 / 123.37 / 166.63 deg, at stream start | **0.187 deg**, at t+2.04 s |
| `hdr_dt` on the largest steps | +1252.60 / -1153.10 / +33.27 / +27.39 s | 0.0049-0.0050 s throughout |
| largest *in-run* step (excluding the jump) | 0.51-1.22 deg | 0.18 deg |
| `yaw_drift.py` `odometry/local`, 60 s parked | — | **+0.04 deg/min** (documented +0.04 / +0.01 / +0.17) |
| `odometry/local` at t+30 s | 5.5e-06 / 8.2e-06 / 3.6e-04 m/s | 1.37e-04 m/s |

The startup discontinuity is gone. The ordinary in-run steps shrank too, from 0.5-1.2 deg to
0.19 deg — the filter had also been integrating the routine 15-45 ms stamp jitter, not just the
pathological startup samples.

`yaw_drift.py` reproduced both gyro biases (VESC +0.004211 rad/s, RealSense -0.002173 rad/s,
against the recorded +0.00434 / -0.00214), and rf2o 0.00 deg/min, VSLAM +0.04 deg/min.
`vehicle/vesc_odom` and `amcl_pose` report `--` as expected with the gate closed and AMCL
motion-gated while parked.

One thing worth naming rather than burying: run17's `/odometry/local` max was 0.023 m/s at
t+143.7 s, higher than run14-16's 0.004-0.008. It is a spike, not a trend — 19 of 6827 samples
exceed 0.005 m/s, and the mean after t+30 s is 8.6e-05 m/s. A desk bump is the likely cause. Two
orders of magnitude below anything resembling bug-244.

Two operational notes for whoever runs these next:

- **`ros2 launch` does not always die on SIGINT here.** All three runs left
  `component_container_isolated` (and once `rviz2`) alive after the group SIGINT and a follow-up
  group TERM. They keep publishing, which would silently contaminate the next launch — every run
  above was preceded by an explicit `kill -9` sweep and a confirmed-empty process list.
- **Do not put `set -u` in a script that sources `/opt/ros/humble/setup.bash`** — it reads an
  unbound `AMENT_TRACE_SETUP_FILES` and the script exits before it launches anything, with
  status 0.
